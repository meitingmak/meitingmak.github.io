## Overview

Photochemical hazes can profoundly influence planetary climates by modifying the absorption and scattering of stellar radiation, as well as the atmospheric temperature structure and circulation.

My research investigates these processes using three-dimensional general circulation models, with a particular focus on the Archean Earth and potentially habitable rocky exoplanets.

## Photochemical Hazes on the Archean Earth

The Archean Earth provides an important analogue for understanding the climates of rocky planets under a faint young Sun.

Using three-dimensional climate simulations, I investigated how prescribed photochemical haze profiles influence atmospheric circulation and surface temperatures.

Our results revealed that optically thin haze can produce surface warming through changes in atmospheric water vapour and cloud feedbacks, while optically thicker haze can lead to substantial cooling.

These findings demonstrate the importance of haze–radiation–dynamics interactions in understanding the climate of early Earth.

## Photochemical Hazes on TRAPPIST-1e

I extended this work to TRAPPIST-1e, a potentially habitable rocky planet orbiting an M-dwarf star.

Through three-dimensional simulations with varying CO2, CH4, and photochemical haze profiles, I investigated how atmospheric composition and haze properties influence surface temperatures and atmospheric circulation.

Our simulations showed that thin haze layers can enhance surface warming under certain conditions, while thicker haze layers generally lead to cooling.

## Implications for Habitability and Observations

Understanding the climatic effects of photochemical hazes is essential for interpreting the atmospheres of potentially habitable exoplanets.

This research provides insights into how aerosols influence surface habitability and how atmospheric composition and climate may affect the interpretation of future observations.
