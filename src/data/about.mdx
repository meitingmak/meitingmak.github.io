Hello! I am a Croucher Postdoctoral Fellow at the University of Oxford, where I study the atmospheres of rocky and gas giant exoplanets, with a particular focus on photochemical hazes, atmospheric dynamics, and their implications for planetary habitability and observations.

My research combines 3D General Circulation Models (GCM), haze and chemistry modelling capabilities, and analytical approaches to investigate how aerosols impact planetary climates and observable spectra. I study a diverse range of planetary environments, from the Archean Earth and potentially habitable rocky exoplanets to hot Jupiters.

A central goal of my research is to understand how atmospheric processes shape our interpretation of exoplanet observations, particularly in the era of the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) and upcoming facilities such as Ariel, as well as future missions targeting potentially habitable worlds such as the Habitable Worlds Observatory (HWO) and the Large Interferometer for Exoplanets (LIFE).

I completed my PhD at the University of Exeter in 2025, where my thesis, *Advancing the Theory of Haze in Exoplanetary Atmospheres*, investigated the role of photochemical hazes in shaping planetary atmospheres.
